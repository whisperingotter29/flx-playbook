02 / Risk · Management & psychology

Size it first

The setup decides where I enter. Risk decides whether I'm still trading next month. Know the contract, size to the stop, and stop trading when the day is done.

1–3% per tradeMin 1:2Max 3 / day

2.1

Know the contract

Points to dollars

Futures let you control a large position with a small margin deposit, which is where the leverage comes from. What matters for sizing is the dollar value of one point. The micro is exactly one tenth of the E-mini, so it's the one to learn on.

ContractPer pointPer tick (0.25)10 points20 points
MES1! · Micro E-mini S&P 500$5$1.25$50$100
ES1! · E-mini S&P 500$50$12.50$500$1,000

Values are per contract. Ten MES move the same dollar amount as one ES, which makes the micro the finer tool for sizing a small account.

Aim for 1:2

Reward to risk

When each winner pays double what a loser costs, I only need to win one trade in three to break even. At 50% I'm clearly profitable. The long and short position tools on the chart show the ratio before I enter.

I risked 4.5 points, about $25. At that size I could lose the same trade around 200 times before the account was gone.

The 1:2 target locked in the gain even though price kept falling afterwards. Taking the planned profit beats guessing how far it will run.

2.4

Target the draw

Risk management, part 2

Price wants to reach liquidity. So instead of a fixed ratio, I aim at areas where price is likely to react. In this long, the take profit sits at a prominent liquidity level (blue line) and the stop sits below another pool of liquidity.

Clearance: when the stop goes at liquidity, leave a few ticks past the level so a wick can't take you out. A candle body shouldn't close in that area.

2.5

Psychology

Stop before greed starts

3 max

Trades per day

Even if I lose all three, three is always the limit. It prevents greed and revenge trading.

+2% stop

One solid winner

If a trade makes 2% or more of the account, I stop for the day. A big enough gain is the sign not to get greedy.
