01 / Concepts · Fundamentals

Read the chart

Five building blocks, each one used by the next. Swing points define structure, structure sets direction, liquidity sits at the swing points, and order blocks and gaps mark where price is likely to react.

Highs & lowsStructureLiquidityOrder blocksIFVG

1.1

Highs and lows

Used to determine trend

High
A green candle followed by a red one. The high is the highest wick of those two candles.
Green → Red
Low
A red candle followed by a green one. The low is the lowest wick of those two candles.
Red → Green

1.2

Market structure

Tells you which direction to enter

If price is trending up, or has bullish market structure, I look to buy. If it's trending down, I look to sell. Structure is read by comparing each new swing to the one before it.

HH · Higher high
Price makes a higher high than the previous high.
Bullish
HL · Higher low
Price makes a higher low than the previous low.
Bullish
LL · Lower low
Price makes a lower low than the previous low.
Bearish
LH · Lower high
Price makes a lower high than the previous high.
Bearish

Trends break all the time, especially on lower timeframes. That's why structure is most useful on higher timeframes, as a filter for which trades to take.

1.3

Liquidity

Resting orders in the market

Liquidity is the stop losses and take profits that haven't been hit yet. Where do people put them? At swing highs and swing lows, the pivot areas of the market. Those levels are where price tends to travel.

Valid swing high
The candle has the highest wick compared with the 2–3 candles on its left and right.
Buy-side liquidity above
Valid swing low
The candle has the lowest wick compared with the 2–3 candles on its left and right.
Sell-side liquidity below

1.4

Order blocks

Where institutions left orders

An order block is the area left behind after a sharp move or a liquidity sweep: the zone where institutional traders placed their buy or sell orders. Mark it from the bottom wick to the top wick of the candle.

Bullish OB
The last down candle before a liquidity sweep.
Look for buys
Bearish OB
The last up candle before a liquidity sweep.
Look for sells

1.5

Inverse FVG

When a gap gets disrespected

Fair value gap
A three-candle pattern where the middle candle isn't overlapped by the wicks of the first or third. It shows an imbalance and a strong move. Price should fill into the gap, then continue in that direction.
Imbalance
Inverse FVG
Price fills the gap but then crosses straight through it instead of continuing. The gap has been disrespected and flips its meaning.
Flipped gap
